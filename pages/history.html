History of Sufism

The history of Sufism is a layered history of devotional movements, teachers, institutions, poetry, scholarship and regional cultures. The timeline below is intentionally selective.

8th–9th c.

Ascetic and devotional roots

Early Muslim ascetics and worshippers developed reputations for intense devotion, renunciation and ethical seriousness.

10th–12th c.

Language of the path

Terms such as maqamat, ahwal, ma'rifa and dhikr became more prominent in spiritual writing.

12th–13th c.

Orders, lodges and scholarship

Institutions and teaching lineages spread, with major figures including Abdul Qadir Gilani, Ibn Arabi and Rumi.

South Asia

Chishtis, Suhrawardis and others

Sufi lineages became influential in the religious and cultural life of South Asia, producing rich traditions of poetry, remembrance and service.

Early modern era

Poetry and vernacular expression

Punjabi, Urdu and other literary cultures carried mystical themes into local languages and performance traditions.

Today

Global readings

Sufi texts circulate in scholarship, translation, devotional communities, music and contemporary conversations on inner life.
