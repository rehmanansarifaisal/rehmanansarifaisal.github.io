Teachers & voices

Famous Sufi Masters

Meet six major figures whose writings, biographies and lineages shaped different regions and centuries of Sufi history.

1207–1273

Rumi

Persian poet and teacher whose Masnavi became one of the most influential works of Persian mystical literature.

WorksMasnavi; Divan-e Shams

Themes: Love, longing, remembrance, the transformation of the self

c. 1680–1757

Bulleh Shah

Punjabi poet associated with the Qadiri tradition and known for vernacular mystical verse.

WorksKafis

Themes: Love, critique of ego and social vanity, direct inward seeking

1077/78–1166

Abdul Qadir Gilani

Hanbali scholar and renowned spiritual teacher whose legacy became associated with the Qadiriyya tradition.

WorksFutuh al-Ghaib; al-Ghunya

Themes: Repentance, discipline, reliance on God, ethical reform

1142–1236

Moinuddin Chishti

Sufi saint traditionally associated with the spread of the Chishti tradition in South Asia, centered in Ajmer.

WorksChishti teaching legacy

Themes: Service, generosity, humility, devotion

1165–1240

Ibn Arabi

Andalusian mystic and prolific author whose writings profoundly influenced later Sufi metaphysics.

WorksFusus al-Hikam; al-Futuhat al-Makkiyya

Themes: Being, divine names, knowledge, the inner meaning of revelation

c. 717–801

Rabia Basri

Early ascetic and devotional figure remembered for teachings and poetry about sincere love of God.

WorksSayings and attributed verses

Themes: Divine love, sincerity, freedom from transactional devotion

Read across traditions

Different teachers. Different vocabularies. A shared seriousness about the heart.

Compare biographies, contexts and texts rather than reducing the tradition to a single voice.
