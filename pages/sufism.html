Understanding Sufism

Sufism is a broad term for Islamic spiritual traditions centered on remembrance, ethical refinement, devotion, love and the purification of the inner life. It has never been a single school with one uniform practice.

Definition

An inward vocabulary.

Sufi writers often speak about nafs (ego/self), qalb (heart), ruh (spirit), dhikr (remembrance), ihsan and spiritual discipline.

Relationship with Islam

Rooted in an Islamic world.

Historically, Sufi movements developed within Islamic religious life, drawing from the Qur’an, Prophetic example, prayer, ethics, scholarship and devotional practice.

The goal

Transformation of character.

The tradition frequently frames spiritual growth through sincerity, humility, remembrance, love, patience and a heart less governed by self-centered impulse.

Plural expressions

Many paths, languages and cultures.

Arab, Persian, Turkic, South Asian and African traditions developed distinctive poetry, institutions and lineages while sharing overlapping spiritual vocabularies.

A four-part map

From early asceticism to modern influence.

Early spiritual traditions

Early Muslim ascetic and devotional figures emphasized fear of God, hope, renunciation, sincerity and the cultivation of character.

Classical Sufi period

From roughly the 10th–13th centuries, Sufi terminology, manuals, biographies and teaching lineages became more systematized.

Famous scholars & poets

Figures including al-Jilani, al-Ghazali, Ibn Arabi and Rumi shaped different dimensions of Sufi thought, literature and practice.

Modern influence

Sufi poetry, shrine cultures, devotional music, scholarship and contemplative practices continue to shape communities around the world.
